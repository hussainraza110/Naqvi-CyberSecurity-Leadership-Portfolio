# Meridian Financial Group — Board Cyber Risk Report
## Q3 2026 | Board Risk Committee

**Cut-off:** 30 September 2026 | **Prepared by:** CISO  
**Classification:** Fictional portfolio scenario

## Executive risk statement
Meridian's cyber risk position is **near the upper boundary of appetite**. Main pressures are privileged identity-control gaps, uneven recovery evidence across critical services, and remediation backlogs for internet-facing assets. Core monitoring and incident-response capabilities remain operational.

**Trajectory:** Modestly deteriorating versus Q2; two appetite indicators are outside tolerance and critical vulnerability remediation duration remains above target.

**Board attention requested:** Endorse 90-day priorities and require monthly evidence-based reporting on privileged-access coverage and critical-service recovery tests. No new risk acceptance is requested.

## Top enterprise risks

### R1 — Privileged identity compromise
Because privileged identities span hybrid platforms and some accounts lack phishing-resistant authentication, an attacker could gain elevated access and disrupt services or expose sensitive information.
- Business impact: service disruption, unauthorized transactions, data exposure, regulatory response.
- Trend: Deteriorating.
- Owner: CIO; CISO provides control oversight.
- Treatment: expand phishing-resistant MFA, reduce standing privilege, improve session monitoring, validate emergency access.
- Board relevance: high-consequence path across critical services.

### R2 — Delayed remediation of critical internet-facing vulnerabilities
Because remediation ownership and maintenance windows vary, exploitable weaknesses may persist beyond tolerance.
- Business impact: outage, customer impact, response cost, potential reporting obligations.
- Trend: Improving, but near tolerance.
- Owner: CIO / technology service owners.
- Treatment: risk-based deadlines, expiring exceptions, compensating-control evidence, executive escalation.

### R3 — Recovery capability not fully evidenced
Because tests do not consistently exercise dependencies and business validation, critical services may not recover within approved objectives during a destructive event.
- Business impact: prolonged banking/payments interruption, customer harm, financial loss.
- Trend: Deteriorating.
- Owner: COO and CIO; CISO provides cyber-scenario assurance.
- Treatment: end-to-end tests, dependency mapping, immutable backup validation, business sign-off.

### R4 — Material supplier assurance gaps
Because selected suppliers have delayed assurance artifacts or incomplete remediation evidence, visibility into supplier compromise or disruption is limited.
- Business impact: outsourced service interruption, data exposure, contractual/regulatory consequences.
- Trend: Stable / near limit.
- Owner: Procurement executive and service owner.
- Treatment: tier-based assurance, remediation commitments, contingency plans, risk-owner disposition.

## Likely board questions

**Are we confident critical services can recover from a destructive cyber event?**  
Not uniformly. Four of six completed tests met the full objective; two require retesting. The gap is treated as an above-appetite resilience issue. Management will report retest evidence, dependencies, and business validation.

**Why are privileged identities still above appetite?**  
Coverage and enforcement are not consistent across the hybrid estate. The plan prioritizes high-impact paths, phishing-resistant MFA, reduced standing access, and monitoring. Monthly updates will show coverage, exceptions, and overdue actions.

**Is additional funding requested today?**  
No incremental approval is requested. Management is reprioritizing within the current plan. If delivery risks show a funding or capacity shortfall, the CISO and CFO will return with quantified options and consequences.
