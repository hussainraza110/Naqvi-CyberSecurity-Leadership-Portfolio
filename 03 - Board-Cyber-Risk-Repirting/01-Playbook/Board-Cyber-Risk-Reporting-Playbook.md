# Board Cyber Risk Reporting Playbook

## Purpose
Give the Board and Board Risk Committee a concise, decision-oriented view of cyber exposure, resilience, material changes, and management action. Translate technical signals into business consequences and governance choices.

## Principles
- Decision-useful: lead with exposure, impact, trajectory, and decisions.
- Risk-based: prioritize material risks and exceptions over activity counts.
- Consistent: preserve metric definitions, thresholds, and reporting periods.
- Transparent: distinguish facts, estimates, assumptions, and unknowns.
- Forward-looking: include scenarios and control sustainability.
- Accountable: assign executive owners and dated actions.
- Proportionate: keep technical detail in appendices or management materials.

## Governance and responsibilities
| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Board | Oversight of strategy, appetite, material exposure, and resilience |
| Board Risk Committee (BRC) | Detailed challenge of position, trends, exceptions, and remediation |
| CEO | Executive accountability for enterprise risk response |
| CISO | Reporting methodology, analysis, and management recommendations |
| CIO / Technology | Technology remediation and service reliability actions |
| CRO / Enterprise Risk | Alignment to enterprise risk taxonomy and appetite |
| CFO | Validate material financial-impact assumptions and investment implications |
| Business executives | Own business impact and treatment decisions |
| Internal Audit | Independent assurance; does not own management controls |

## Cadence
- Quarterly: Board/BRC pack covering position, trend, material risks, resilience, investment, and decisions.
- Monthly: executive cyber risk review; validate metric quality, owners, overdue actions, and breaches.
- Event-driven: prompt escalation of material incidents, appetite breaches, critical control failures, or resilience concerns.
- Annual: reconfirm appetite, thresholds, taxonomy, and board information needs.

## Standard board-pack structure
1. Executive risk statement and overall trajectory
2. Appetite status and material exceptions
3. Top enterprise cyber risks and business impact
4. Critical-service resilience and recovery readiness
5. Threat and exposure developments relevant to the enterprise
6. Control and remediation performance
7. Investment / capacity implications
8. Decisions and management commitments
9. Appendix: definitions, metric details, assumptions, caveats

## Workflow
1. Set cut-off date and reporting scope.
2. Collect inputs from accountable owners.
3. Validate source, completeness, period, and denominator.
4. Compare actuals to appetite and tolerance.
5. Analyze trend, concentration, and business consequence.
6. Challenge narratives with business, technology, and risk owners.
7. Obtain CISO and CRO review; CFO input for financial estimates.
8. Finalize narrative and decision requests.
9. Present; record challenge and decisions.
10. Track actions and close the loop next cycle.

## Escalation
Escalate outside the quarterly cycle when a threshold is breached; a critical service is materially disrupted or recovery objectives are at risk; an incident may have material customer, regulatory, financial, or operational implications; a critical control failure creates immediate material exposure; or an exception exceeds delegated authority or expires.

Initial notification should state what happened, affected services/data, known business impact, containment, uncertainty, accountable executive, next update, and decision needed. Do not wait for complete forensic certainty; label knowns and unknowns.

## Risk narrative standard
Each material risk answers:
- Risk statement: Because of [cause], [event] may affect [objective/service], resulting in [business consequence].
- Exposure and affected scope
- Trajectory and drivers
- Controls and evidence
- Treatment, owner, due date
- Residual risk and decision required

## Quality gates
- Metric definitions, cut-off, and denominators are visible.
- RAG status follows explicit thresholds.
- Trend is not inferred from a single point.
- Risk narratives avoid unsupported precision.
- Actions have role owners, due dates, and closure evidence.
- Decision requests state options, recommendation, consequences of deferral, and authority.
- Sensitive technical details are appropriately restricted.
