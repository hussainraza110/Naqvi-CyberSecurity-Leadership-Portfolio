# Worked Example — Q3 2026 Reporting Cycle

## Scenario
Meridian Financial Group is a fictional regulated financial-services enterprise with banking, digital payments, investment services, a hybrid technology estate, and critical third-party dependencies. All values, events, thresholds, and conclusions are invented for demonstration.

## Inputs and accountable owners
| Input | Owner | Validation |
|---|---|---|
| Privileged identity coverage | CIO / IAM lead | Population, enforcement, exceptions |
| Vulnerability remediation | Technology service owners | Scope, due dates, exceptions |
| Recovery testing | COO / resilience office | Test scope, objectives, business acceptance |
| Detection and response SLA | CISO / SOC lead | Severity and timing definitions |
| Supplier assurance | Procurement / service owners | Supplier tier and evidence freshness |
| Financial impact | CFO and business owners | Assumptions and ranges |

## Synthesis
The enterprise remains near the upper boundary of appetite. Privileged identity and critical-service resilience are above appetite. Vulnerability remediation is improving but near its limit. Detection and response is within appetite; supplier assurance is near limit.

The key distinction is between control activity and risk reduction. Higher MFA coverage and more tests are positive, but residual risk remains elevated until coverage is complete and recovery outcomes meet approved objectives.

## Management decision logic
1. Prioritize privileged identities reaching critical services and sensitive data.
2. Reduce standing privilege and strengthen emergency-access monitoring.
3. Retest the two critical services that did not demonstrate full recovery objectives.
4. Keep vulnerability exceptions time-bound with compensating controls and service owners.
5. Obtain current assurance and remediation plans from material suppliers with overdue evidence.
6. Report monthly to executive risk governance; escalate material developments immediately.

## Board challenge log
| Challenge | Response | Follow-up evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is residual risk accepted? | No new acceptance requested; existing remediation plan remains in effect. | Risk register and exception status |
| What triggers immediate escalation? | Material incident, critical service disruption, significant appetite breach, or recovery-objective failure. | Event notification and decision log |
| How is remediation effectiveness demonstrated? | Evidence-based coverage, control testing, retests, and business owner validation. | Test artifacts and metric lineage |
| What if deadlines slip? | Escalate; return with capacity/funding trade-offs and residual-risk implications. | Updated forecast and decision paper |

## Conclusion
The BRC is asked to endorse management priorities and reinforce service-owner accountability—not approve an unbounded risk acceptance. Management provides monthly progress evidence and escalates material events through established channels.
