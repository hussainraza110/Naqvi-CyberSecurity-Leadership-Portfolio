# Risk-Based Prioritization Methodology

## Scoring dimensions
Score each factor from 1 (low) to 5 (very high). The weighted score is an aid to portfolio discussion.

| Factor | Weight | Guidance |
|---|---:|---|
| Inherent risk severity | 25% | Potential impact and likelihood before proposed uplift |
| Control gap / exposure | 20% | Evidence of weakness, exploitable exposure, or missing control |
| Business criticality | 20% | Importance of affected service, data, and customer outcomes |
| Regulatory / contractual urgency | 15% | Deadline, obligation, audit commitment, or enforcement exposure |
| Risk-reduction confidence | 10% | Strength of evidence that the proposal will reduce the scenario |
| Delivery readiness | 10% | Feasibility, ownership, dependencies, and capacity |

**Weighted score** = sum of (dimension score × weight). Maximum = 5.00.

## Suggested bands
- 4.20–5.00: Priority 1 — executive attention / near-term action
- 3.50–4.19: Priority 2 — plan and fund in current cycle where feasible
- 2.70–3.49: Priority 3 — sequence based on capacity and dependencies
- Below 2.70: Priority 4 — defer, redesign, or monitor

## Mandatory override
A legal, regulatory, contractual, or formally committed remediation deadline is not displaced by a lower weighted score. Mark it **Mandatory** and document the obligation, accountable owner, and due date.

## Example
For a privileged-access uplift: severity 5, control gap 5, criticality 5, urgency 4, confidence 4, readiness 3.

Score = 5×0.25 + 5×0.20 + 5×0.20 + 4×0.15 + 4×0.10 + 3×0.10 = **4.60 (Priority 1)**.

## Challenge and safeguards
Scores require evidence and a named assessor. Record uncertainty and sensitivity; avoid false precision. A high score is not an automatic approval, and a low score is not permission to accept risk. Re-score when facts or assumptions change.
