# Cybersecurity Capability and Maturity Model

This is a strategic prioritization tool, not an audit rating or certification.

## Scale
1 Initial (ad hoc); 2 Developing (partly repeatable); 3 Defined (documented and consistently implemented); 4 Managed (measured and integrated); 5 Adaptive (continually improved). Target maturity is risk-driven.

## Domains and illustrative scores
| Domain | Baseline | Year-3 target | Rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Strategy, governance, risk | 2.5 | 4.0 | Risk ownership and executive decisions |
| Architecture / engineering | 2.5 | 3.5 | Patterns and exception control |
| Identity / access | 2.0 | 4.0 | Identity and privileged exposure |
| Security operations / response | 3.0 | 4.0 | Scenario detection and response |
| Application / cloud security | 2.0 | 3.5 | Secure delivery |
| Data protection | 2.5 | 3.5 | Sensitive-data protection |
| Third-party security | 2.0 | 3.5 | Critical supplier and concentration risk |
| Cyber resilience | 2.0 | 4.0 | Recovery of critical services |
| Workforce / culture | 2.5 | 3.5 | Role-based enablement |
| Performance / improvement | 2.0 | 3.5 | Outcome and evidence-based reviews |

All values are illustrative; do not mechanically average them as a validated enterprise score.

## Assessment method
Define outcomes and scope; collect policy/process/system/interview/exercise evidence; score observable criteria; record confidence and limitations; assess business impact; agree risk-based target; assign owner, actions, dependencies, and review date; validate with business, technology, risk, and assurance.

Prioritize by critical-service impact, scenario plausibility, obligations, appetite, dependencies, feasibility, capacity, and cost. A maturity gap alone does not justify investment.
