# Executive Decision Scenarios

Fictional scenarios demonstrate reasoning; they are not universal prescriptions.

## 1. Budget reduction
CFO asks for a 15% cut while digital payments expand. Reassess risk and obligations; separate foundational from discretionary work; present full, constrained, and risk-acceptance options; show schedule/residual-risk consequences; obtain an authorized decision. Ask: “What risk changes if we defer, and who owns it?”

## 2. Business rejects security requirement
Clarify threat and outcome; consider alternative controls; assess customer, regulatory, product, and operational impacts; document residual risk, owner, and expiry; escalate beyond appetite. Do not silently waive or claim risk ownership.

## 3. Critical supplier weakness
Validate service dependency, access, exposure, compensating controls, and concentration. Engage service owner, procurement, legal, risk, supplier management. Consider monitoring, restrictions, contingency, or alternate service. Set evidence, milestones, and authorized acceptance.

## 4. Board requests one maturity score
Explain composite scores can hide exposure and evidence limits. Present risk themes/trends, resilience, domain maturity, exceptions/owners, outcomes, and decisions. If using a composite, document weighting, confidence, and limits.

## 5. Incident volume rises
Do not infer posture deterioration automatically. Separate telemetry, event volume, confirmed incidents, severity, response performance, and business impact; explain improved visibility and investigate risk trend.

## 6. Acquisition announced
Establish integration governance; map services, data, identity, and third parties; prioritize material exposure; define target architecture and operating model; sequence around continuity and risk; refresh strategy, funding, and workforce.
