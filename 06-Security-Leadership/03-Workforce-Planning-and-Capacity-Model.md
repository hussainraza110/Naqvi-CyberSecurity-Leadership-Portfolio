# Workforce Planning and Capacity Model

## Method
1. Define services and service-level expectations.
2. Estimate workload drivers (assets, alerts, releases, suppliers, incidents).
3. Establish demand and complexity assumptions.
4. Assess capacity, skills, coverage, and resilience.
5. Compare hire, develop, automate, outsource, or simplify options.
6. Phase actions and review quarterly.

**Net capacity** = paid hours − leave − training − administration − planned non-delivery time. **Demand gap** = forecast workload hours − net capacity. Use operational evidence; do not assume all paid time is delivery time.

| Capability | Driver | Planning action |
|---|---|---|
| SOC | Alert volume and coverage hours | Tune, automate, then staff |
| GRC | Assessments, audits, risk reviews | Standardize evidence |
| AppSec | Teams, releases, tests | Risk-tier coverage and self-service |
| IAM | Identity transactions and reviews | Workflow automation |
| IR | Incidents and on-call coverage | Cross-train and surge support |

Hire builds durable skills but takes time; development retains context; automation scales repeatable work; managed service adds capacity with dependency; simplification removes demand. Track single-person dependencies and fatigue.
