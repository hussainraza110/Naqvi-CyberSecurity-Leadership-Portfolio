# Cyber Crisis Management Playbook

## Purpose
Coordinate enterprise decisions when a cyber event threatens critical services, customers, financial/operational stability, obligations, or reputation. This playbook governs crisis leadership; technical handling is in the incident response plan.

## Activation triggers
Activate when a critical service is materially disrupted or recovery objectives may be exceeded; an incident may cause significant customer, financial, operational, legal, or regulatory impact; shared identity/core payment infrastructure is compromised; executive cross-functional decisions are needed; or the Incident Commander / executive on-call judges ordinary incident governance insufficient. Do not wait for complete forensic certainty.

## Command roles
| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| Executive Crisis Lead (CEO/delegate) | Enterprise priorities, risk decisions, resources, board escalation |
| Crisis Manager | Cadence, SITREPs, action and decision logs |
| Incident Commander | Technical response objectives and coordination |
| CISO | Cyber assessment, response strategy, threat/control expertise |
| CIO / Technology Lead | Containment, restoration, engineering capacity |
| COO / Business Service Lead | Service/customer impact, continuity, business validation |
| CRO | Risk and appetite implications |
| General Counsel / Legal | Legal, privacy, notification advice |
| Communications Lead | Stakeholder messaging and approvals |
| CFO | Financial impact and cost estimates |
| Third-Party Lead | Supplier coordination and external dependencies |

## Cadence and decisions
During active high-tempo response, consider executive updates every 30–60 minutes (illustrative); adjust to impact and decision need. Crisis leadership decides enterprise trade-offs; technical leads may take pre-authorized protective actions. Record decisions affecting customer service, data integrity, obligations, or appetite.

Decision examples: isolate a critical service/shared identity component; invoke continuity; restore from backup; engage external response support; approve communications; accept service restrictions or time-bound residual risk.

## SITREP minimum fields
Timestamp/classification; summary; confirmed facts and confidence; unknowns; affected services/systems/data; customer/financial/operational impact; completed and ongoing actions; containment/recovery posture; decisions required; owner and next update.

## Logs
Keep authoritative timestamps, decision rationale/approver, actions/owners/dates/evidence, assumptions and validation, external contacts, communications, service impact, and recovery milestones.

## Stand-down
Require containment criteria, critical-service restoration or accepted continuity, security/integrity/business validation, assigned residual risks, tracked communications, and Executive Crisis Lead approval. Schedule post-incident review. Stand-down does not mean every technical action is closed.
