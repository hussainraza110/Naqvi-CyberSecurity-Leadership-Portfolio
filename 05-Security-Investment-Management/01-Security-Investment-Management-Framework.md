# Security Investment Management Framework

## 1. Objective
Establish a transparent lifecycle for identifying, evaluating, approving, delivering, and measuring cybersecurity investments. The framework helps executives compare proposals in business and risk terms.

## 2. Lifecycle
| Stage | Required activity | Decision/output |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | Capture risk, obligation, incident, audit, strategy, or lifecycle driver | Intake record |
| Assess | Establish baseline risk, control gap, scope, and affected services | Risk statement and evidence |
| Shape | Compare options, including process, architecture, managed service, and no-change | Options analysis |
| Quantify | Estimate implementation and recurring costs; identify benefits and uncertainty | Business case |
| Prioritize | Apply scoring, dependencies, mandatory status, and capacity constraints | Ranked portfolio |
| Approve | Route to delegated authority; record conditions and funding | Decision record |
| Deliver | Manage milestones, change, procurement, and control implementation | Delivery evidence |
| Realize | Measure outcomes against baseline and target | Benefits review |
| Reassess | Refresh assumptions, residual risk, and strategic fit | Continue, change, or retire |

## 3. Investment classes
- **Regulatory / mandatory:** required by law, regulator, contract, or formally accepted remediation.
- **Risk reduction:** addresses material threat scenarios or control deficiencies.
- **Resilience:** reduces disruption duration or improves recovery confidence.
- **Strategic enablement:** supports secure digital growth, cloud adoption, or product delivery.
- **Technical lifecycle:** replaces unsupported, fragile, or uneconomic capabilities.
- **Efficiency / optimization:** consolidates capabilities or reduces operational effort.

## 4. Minimum proposal evidence
A proposal should include: accountable sponsor; business service and risk owner; risk scenario; current controls and evidence; options considered; scope and exclusions; one-time and recurring costs; dependencies; delivery capacity; measurable outcomes; residual risk; and a proposed review date.

## 5. Guardrails
- Risk scores support judgment; they do not override legal requirements or executive accountability.
- Benefits must not be double-counted across initiatives.
- Avoid presenting modeled loss reduction as guaranteed cash savings.
- Include operating costs, integration, migration, training, support, and exit costs in TCO.
- Any exception must have an owner, rationale, expiry date, and monitoring plan.

## 6. Roles
| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| CISO | Portfolio owner; risk-informed recommendation |
| Business / service owner | Business outcome and operational adoption |
| Risk owner | Risk acceptance and residual-risk accountability |
| Finance | Cost validation, budget control, benefit challenge |
| Architecture | Design fit, integration, technical debt and dependencies |
| Procurement / Legal | Commercial, vendor, privacy and contractual review |
| Investment committee | Prioritization and delegated funding decisions |
| Delivery owner | Scope, schedule, implementation evidence and reporting |

## 7. Review cadence
Monthly portfolio review; quarterly executive investment review; annual strategy and budget refresh; and event-driven reassessment after material incidents, regulatory change, acquisitions, or major architecture shifts.
