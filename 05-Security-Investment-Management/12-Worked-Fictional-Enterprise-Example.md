# Worked Fictional Enterprise Example

## Enterprise context
Meridian Financial Group is a fictional regulated financial services organization providing banking, digital payments, and investment services through a hybrid technology environment. This example is invented for demonstration; it does not describe an actual employer or institution.

## Portfolio challenge
The enterprise has limited investment capacity and competing needs: privileged identity risk, incomplete security telemetry, recovery uncertainty for critical services, sensitive-data exposure, and inconsistent secure development practices.

## Executive choice
The illustrative committee selects a balanced portfolio rather than allocating the full budget to a single platform:
- Identity / PAM and critical service recovery are treated as Priority 1 because of severe business-service impact.
- Detection and exposure management are sequenced as high-priority risk reduction.
- Data protection and secure delivery are staged around discovery and product-owner readiness.
- Third-party uplift and role-based awareness are retained as enabling controls with measurable milestones.

## Example decision record
**Decision:** Approve the first-year portfolio envelope of PKR 338m, subject to initiative-level business cases and staged gates.  
**Conditions:** Finance confirms estimate basis; sponsors validate outcomes; architecture reviews material designs; mandatory commitments are tracked separately; quarterly reviews can reallocate funds where evidence changes.  
**Risk posture:** Funding approval does not itself establish that risk is within appetite. Residual risk is assessed after controls are implemented and tested.

## What executives should see
A concise board-level view: material cyber scenarios, how the investments change exposure or resilience, spend and forecast, delivery confidence, unmet outcomes, and decisions required. Tool deployment counts alone are insufficient.
